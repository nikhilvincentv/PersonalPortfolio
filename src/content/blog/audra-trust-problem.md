After the hackathon, I wanted to see if [Audra](/blog/audra-ai-tax-agent) could get real users. My goal was 50 paying online sellers and about $1,000 in monthly revenue by mid-November. My plan was simple: go where sellers hang out, offer a free tax audit, and let the product speak for itself.

Before posting anything, I read the actual rules of every community I planned to use. That killed the plan.

## Every seller community bans what I was about to do

- **r/ecommerce** forbids trying to solicit or contact users "in any way (even if free)." My free audit offer broke the rule in its own wording.
- **r/shopify** prohibits self-promotion and bans repeat offenders.
- **r/AmazonSeller** bans your account **and** blacklists your domain.
- **r/Flipping** confines promotion to weekly threads.
- Most **Facebook groups** run posts through an approval queue. The generous ones allow one promo a month with admin permission.
- On **Discord**, unsolicited promotional DMs break Discord's own terms of service.

If I'd gone with my first plan, I'd have been banned in week one, and worse, I'd have taught every seller community to associate the name Audra with spam.

## The bigger problem was trust

Even if the rules allowed it, picture what the post looks like from the other side. An anonymous account asks you to connect your bank to an AI tool you've never heard of. That's the exact pattern these communities have learned to report as a scam. Being 17 makes it worse.

You can't fix that with better copy. A tax product lives or dies on trust, and you earn trust slowly or not at all.

## What I'm doing instead

I split my channels into two kinds.

**Earn-trust channels** are Reddit, Facebook groups, and Discord. The plan there is no promotion at all. Just answering questions about 1099-Ks, quarterly estimates, and what counts as a deduction. The common guideline is 90/10: at least nine genuinely useful posts for every mention of what you built, and some seller subs are closer to 99/1. If I ever do mention Audra, I'll say "full disclosure, I built this," because getting caught hiding it means a permanent ban and a community that turns on you.

**Promo-native channels** are X, TikTok, and reaching out to sellers through their own public storefronts. Building in public is normal on X and TikTok, and messaging a business through its own contact page isn't community spam. I expect most early users to come from here.

A few rules I wrote for myself:

- One account. No second account to upvote or vouch for myself. Reddit catches it and the ban is sitewide.
- Never post the same message to multiple subs on the same day.
- No marketing words. "Revolutionary" and "seamless" get posts removed and make people stop reading.
- Put my real name and face on it. An anonymous fintech tool reads as a scam. A named student with a public GitHub at least reads as a person.

## What I learned

I spent a weekend building Audra and assumed distribution would be the easy part. It's turning out to be harder than the code, and slower. Reading the rules first took an afternoon and probably saved the project. If you're about to launch something into an online community, open the sidebar and read the rules before you write a single post.
